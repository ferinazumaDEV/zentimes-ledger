How "substance" is graded: the reference sample
n = 56 · measured on 2026-09-23 · sample pymes-es-2026-09. Only n, date, method and values are published, never the sites' names or addresses.
Method
Words in sentences (blocks of 10 words or more) readable without JavaScript in the initial HTML of the home page, excluding menu, footer, cookie banner and hidden text, measured with the same reader the inspector uses on 56 home pages of Spanish small businesses downloaded once on 23 Sep 2026 (convenience sample; 2 of 58 returned 403 and are excluded).
Cut-offs
None: under 250 characters in sentences (absolute; a convention taken from trafilatura's minimum extraction size, not a search engine rule).
Low: below p25 (295 words).
Good: p25 to p75 (295–949).
Strong: p75 to p92 (949–1405).
Excellent: p92 or above (1405), and also readable text (Readability), under 15% repeated sentence blocks and no content finding. That is the top 8%, like Lighthouse's green: Lighthouse puts its 50 at the 25th percentile of HTTP Archive and its 90 at the 8th, for metrics where less is better; with words more is better, so their p25 and p8 are our p75 and p92.
Percentiles by linear interpolation. Within 5% of a cut the card says "on the edge" and the label does not change. Only Spanish home pages are labelled with this curve; there is no inner-page sample yet, so articles, offers and other pages only get minimums and findings.

How each finding lowers a label
The label is the lower of what the rule gives and the cap of the worst open finding. Requirements are only Met / Not met / Not applicable: a failure is a finding, not a grade. Criteria history
v2026-09-24
·
2026-09-24
—
The criteria now start from defects. Technical requirements (response, HTTPS, indexing, robots, encoding, viewport, canonical, language, sitemap, hreflang…) are met or not; if not, they raise a finding with a severity: critical, serious, moderate or minor. Only substance (words in sentences readable without JavaScript, excluding menu, footer, cookie banner and hidden text) is graded up to "excellent", by percentile of 56 home pages of Spanish small businesses (sample published with n, date, method and values, without names): "excellent" is only the top 8%, like Lighthouse's green, and also requires readable text, under 15% repeated blocks and no content finding. Site depth is "not rated" until there is an inner-page sample. Everything else is capped at "good": having no defects is expected, not a merit. Every open defect lowers its card's label (convention, by analogy with WCAG). What is required depends on the page type: a contact page is not measured in words. Up to 6 more pages of the site are checked, chosen deterministically, with at most 24 requests to the origin, one at a time, 1 s apart, stopping if the server asks; that part arrives later, with a progress indicator. Back doors are closed: a "HOME" title, no h1, a one-item BreadcrumbList counted as complete, skip links as anchors, a soft 404 that did not lower anything, an owner only linked. What this inspector cannot see (layout, contrast, a covering banner, console errors) is reviewed in a browser by the free mini-audit. Also: if the page does not return 2xx (a block on the inspector, a 404), what arrives is not analysed as if it were your page; when the page's lang contradicts the language of the text, the text wins and the wrong lang is a separate finding; text that React streams in hidden has its own finding; WordPress sample content counts as filler; a WordPress contact page and legal notice are no longer taken for articles. On names: until now the version number was a sequence, not a month (the previous seven were all published on 2026-09-23); from this one on, the name is the publication date. Results are not comparable with reports made under earlier versions. Many sites will drop without having changed: what changed is the criteria. Added the same day as an INTERFACE change, not a criteria change (no label moves): every card with data-value declares what that number is in data-value-kind (words, count, broken, bytes, http-status, per-1000-words), and those giving a part of a total also carry data-total. For outbound links and anchors, data-value counts the broken ones while the screen shows the total; now nobody has to guess.
